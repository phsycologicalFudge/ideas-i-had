"The existence of truth is independent of the ability to observe and understand it"

### The start

If observation collapses a quantum state, could consciousness be a fundamental force in reality, bridging physics and philosophy?

### The laboratory thought experiment

A scientist sits inside a sealed laboratory and observes a particle. They record a definite result. Wigner is outside the laboratory and has not yet observed anything. From the scientist's side, the experiment has a definite result. From Wigner's side, should the whole laboratory, scientist included, still be described as a superposition?

### Truth: It's two sides

Reality's truth exists whether or not anyone is aware of it. The statement "particle x is 1" can be true or false. Particle x doesn't have to be 1, it can be 0. Whichever it is, that value is the truth, and it exists regardless. Observed truth is what an observer gets from an observation, and it depends on the exact scenario it was observed in. Two people can approach the same problem and reach different conclusions, so what actually exists and what is known or thought to exist are separate concepts.

The observer changes what is understood about the observed, not the observed object/state itself.

### Wigner's superposition

For Wigner, the superposition is real. But it is a state that is independent of reality's truth. It could even be reality's truth, but Wigner is not able to decide that. He can only say the state exists there and then. Why? Because as a human observer reliant on reality, he is unable to grasp its fundamental truths. This is more philosophical than scientific, perhaps.

### What counts as an observer

An observer doesn't need to be alive. Any tool that touches the object has observed it. What the tool cannot do is decide whether it has witnessed a truth. It is programmed to do something, with no understanding, so it cannot hold a truth even though the truth exists. The people using the tool are able to hold the truth however, which means they become an observer capable of holding the observed truth.

### Extra: The principle of independent truth

An important nuance to see, is that the rigidness of an assumed reality is in itself an open possibility: it can either be true or false. An example is:

Particle X could be any of {s1,s2,s3}.

The assumption that x can either be 1 of 3 states and either be it or not, is still an observed truth once confirmed. Before confirmation there is no observed truth, instead we use maths and reasoning based on what's known to deduce what isn't known. The issue is that line of thought can break because what is not observed is not known. So even the most rigid maths can fail to describe reality, which makes the assumption it relied on false once that truth is observed.
