### What it means to think

I had always seen philosophy as a way to overthink. To create complexity where there is none. In fact, upon searching for the definition, one will be met with:

"Philosophy is the study or creation of theories about basic things such as the nature of existence, knowledge, and thought, or about how people should live."

From this, an interesting thought arose. Is the creation of thought itself philosophy? What must one do to be 'philosophical'?

Do you see it? Merely thinking about thought was, in fact, the very act of philosophy. We live our lives thinking, planning and creating. And so I asked myself one simple question:

"How should we think?"

If you were to watch two children grow in the same environment, the same house even, the odds are they will share opinions, though naturally we would expect some differences.

Now take two other children, and place one in the midst of war, and another in a house of riches and joy. Will they both grow with the same opinions? Will they think the same? Will their philosophies be similar?

Probably not. Who's to say. See, the beauty and curse of philosophy is the fact that it is shared. We form opinions and gain knowledge through communication. Those we meet, the histories we form, it all contributes to one's philosophy.

How an individual thinks.

### Chain Philosophy

If how an individual thinks is their philosophy, then it is important to maintain the ability to express and communicate a thought. However, I often find it difficult to express a complex amalgamation of emotion, experience and knowledge in one coherent sentence.

The solution, however, was there, in front of me. To understand what Chain Philosophy is, it is important to understand what a chain is. In earlier examples, we worked through a simulation of two groups of children — different histories and different outcomes. To achieve this, we created the foundation on a blank canvas: their ability to think. From that stemmed the only thing capable of changing it, time. That is a chain. We started with a foundation, whether right or wrong, that exists as something someone can identify.

Then came the natural progression, evident in the average growth of a human being, paired with learned understandings such as the consequence of similarity (growing together). This produced the first chain: a child who grows, learning from their surroundings, and eventually forming similar opinions.

We then returned to the foundation and changed a variable. For that exercise, we changed the environment of the two children, naturally forming a different chain than the first.

Do you see it?

Both chains stem from the same foundation, yet produce different results. To think is not the same as to think correctly. Most times we will find that no matter how much we reason, the truth may still be unreachable.

What matters is not the conclusion, but the act of getting to said conclusion.

### Certainty vs Uncertainty

If you were to walk through a park and suddenly heard the cries of an animal stuck in a tree, what would you do? Would you save it? Or perhaps call for help?

Let us assume another person was in said scenario, and had the ability to climb trees with ease. Some of us would urge them to save the poor creature. Yet, what happens if they are uncertain? Perhaps the tree lacks enough support, or they are worried about a branch breaking.

Whatever their worry is, it has superseded their ability to think. They are no longer thinking of the chain before them, and, in effect, are lost in the uncertainty of the truth.

Likewise, had that person been fully certain in their abilities or luck, only to be left stranded due to broken branches or unexpected wildlife living in dense areas, then their certainty had led them to the falsehood of hope — a false truth they would not have known until it was too late.

You might be wondering how this is important to Chain Philosophy... or maybe you're not...

My conclusion is that the certainty of uncertainty creates a beautiful paradox, where either end of the extreme should not decide your choices in life.

### The Paradox

As the examples have shown, Chain Philosophy might eventually lead one to question why and how certainty in the uncertain could make any sense. Is it not a declaration of certainty in something? That must surely be the very thing warned against.

This is true; certainty in the uncertain could indeed be interpreted that way. However, this is what I'll call the ambiguity of the English language.

There are two distinct meanings: to be certain 'of' x, and to be certain 'within' x.

To be certain of uncertainty, one must be within the uncertainty, otherwise it becomes the subject of which they are sure, rather than the evidence itself.

An example of 'within' would be Person X and Person B. If Person X has to make a choice about Person B, but has conflicting reports on Person B, then Person X will be unsure. Yet, certainty in just being 'unsure' does not produce an answer. It merely acknowledges one's own limits, like the first meaning.

To make a decision, they must be neither certain nor uncertain without reasoning. For example, Person X might not know if Person B is truly a bad person, but their own knowledge points to a real crime that has happened, while the other alleged crimes remain uncertain.

A judgement is therefore possible. In the certainty of their uncertainty, a judgement based on their reasoning in the present, not emotion, has been passed.

In other words, the extreme ends of the line can lead to a loss of control, whilst the ability to remain in the middle grants clarity of mind where there is fog.

That is the certainty of uncertainty, combined with Chain Philosophy.
